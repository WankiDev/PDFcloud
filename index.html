    How to merge PDF files securely online

    Combine contracts, receipts or applications into one file without handing them to a stranger’s server.

    • Arrange files in order before merging
    • Check the result before deleting originals
    • Avoid tools that ask for your email
    Read the full guide

    Combining several PDF files into a single document is one of the most common office tasks, whether you're assembling a contract packet, a school application, or a set of scanned receipts for an expense report. The trouble with most "free" merge tools online is that they ask you to upload your files to a remote server first — which means your bank statement, medical form, or signed agreement briefly leaves your control and lands on infrastructure you have no visibility into. For anything containing personal or financial information, that's a real risk, not a theoretical one.

    PDFCloud's Merge tool takes a different approach. When you drop your files into the workspace, they are read directly into your browser's memory using JavaScript's File API. The actual page-combining work is handled by pdf-lib, an open-source library that runs entirely client-side. At no point does a file leave your device — there is no upload progress bar because there is no upload. You can verify this yourself by opening your browser's network inspector and watching that no file data is transmitted while the merge happens.

    To merge securely, start by gathering the files you need in the order you want them to appear. PDFCloud lets you drag and drop each entry in the file list to rearrange the final sequence before you commit, so there's no need to pre-sort files on your desktop first. Once you're happy with the order, the tool stitches every page from every document into one continuous PDF and hands you a download link immediately.

    A good habit, regardless of which tool you use, is to check the final merged file before deleting your originals, and to avoid pasting sensitive documents into any tool that requires you to create an account or accept a lengthy data-sharing agreement first. If a "free" PDF tool asks for your email before it will let you download your own file, that's usually a sign your document was uploaded somewhere you can't see.

    Why file compression matters for job portals

    Get large scans under the size limits of job portals and government forms.

    • Use Light for small print and diagrams
    • Max works well for plain text documents
    • Zoom in to confirm signatures stay legible
    Read the full guide

    Almost every job application portal, government form submission, and university admissions system enforces a file size limit — often somewhere between 1MB and 5MB. It's a frustrating discovery: you've scanned your resume, transcripts, or a signed offer letter, only to find the resulting PDF is 12MB because your scanner or phone camera defaulted to a high-resolution capture mode. The upload fails, the form gives you a vague error, and you're left hunting for a way to shrink the file without destroying its legibility.

    Compression works by reducing the amount of data needed to represent the images embedded inside a PDF. A scanned page is usually stored as a large bitmap image, and most of that image's fine detail — background texture on the paper, faint scanner noise — contributes very little to how readable the document is. By re-encoding those images at a lower resolution and a more efficient compression quality, the file size can often drop by 60-90% while the text remains perfectly readable on screen or in print.

    PDFCloud's Compress tool renders each page of your PDF onto a canvas at a controlled resolution and re-saves it as a new, lighter document, all inside your browser tab. You can choose between Light, Recommended, and Max compression depending on how aggressively you need to shrink the file — Light preserves more fine detail for documents with small print or fine diagrams, while Max is built for straightforward text documents where file size is the priority.

    Before submitting any compressed document to a job portal or official system, it's worth opening the result once to confirm any signatures, stamps, or small print remain legible. If a document is compressed too aggressively, some detail can be lost — which is exactly why PDFCloud gives you a choice of compression levels rather than applying a single fixed setting.

    How image-to-PDF conversion works

    Turn photos and scans into one tidy, multi-page PDF.

    • Each image becomes its own page
    • Choose A4 or Letter page sizing
    • Straighten and crop crooked scans first
    Read the full guide

    Converting a photo or scanned image into a PDF is one of those tasks that seems simple until you actually need to do it — a landlord wants your ID as a PDF, a professor wants homework photos combined into one file, or you need to submit a set of receipt photos as a single document rather than five separate JPGs. Image formats like JPG and PNG are built to display a single picture, while PDF is a document format designed to hold one or more pages in a fixed, printable layout — which is exactly why the conversion step exists.

    Under the hood, converting an image to a PDF page involves creating a new blank page sized to match the image's dimensions, then embedding the image data onto that page at the correct position and scale. PDFCloud's Image to PDF tool handles this automatically: each image you add becomes its own page, sized to fit the picture cleanly, and multiple images are combined into a single multi-page PDF in the order you arrange them.

    Because this conversion happens using your browser's own image-decoding and canvas capabilities together with the pdf-lib library, there's no meaningful limit on how many images you can combine, aside from your device's available memory. Very large batches of high-resolution photos will naturally take a little longer to process, since your device is doing all the work that a remote server would otherwise handle — but nothing ever has to leave your browser tab to get there.

    If you're combining scanned pages that are slightly crooked or inconsistently lit, it's worth straightening and cropping them in a photo editor first, since PDFCloud places each image as-is without automatic rotation or cropping. For most everyday needs — ID copies, receipts, quick homework scans — dropping the images in, checking the order, and converting takes only a few seconds.
